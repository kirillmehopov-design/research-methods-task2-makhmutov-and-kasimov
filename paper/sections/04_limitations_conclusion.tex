\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
The 33-person convenience sample, Java maintenance tasks, and source-study
Copilot setting limit generalization. This exploratory secondary analysis was
planned after inspecting the released data and findings. Experience groups
were not randomized; task differences and unmodeled session period may
contribute to the observed contrast. Equal task-allocation weighting does not
resolve period or carryover effects. Completion captures task requirements,
not comprehensive code quality or security, and bootstrap intervals do not
remove these design limitations.

Automatic suggestions were associated with higher novice productivity
(RQ1) and lower expert productivity (RQ2). The direct difference in changes
was $-0.5660$ task fraction/hour (RQ3), with a 95\% CI below zero. Its sign
persisted across participant-removal and task-allocation checks. Novice gains
accompanied greater completion and longer sessions. Our contribution connects
published measurements to validated pairs, explicit estimates, and reproducible
figures. A next study should repeat the paired mode comparison on additional
tasks, record first/second-session labels, and assess solution quality alongside
completion and time.
